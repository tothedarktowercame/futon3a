Two solutions, compared — backward (mine) vs Alexander (forward)

do the two methods give qualitatively different answers to the same sorry? Yes.

Same spec (cascade-dict → (Wiring, [PolicyHole])), two derivations. Backward: I built the wiring then back-tagged patterns (works only because this was a toy I thought I could see). Forward (Alexander): restate the sorry as IF/HOWEVER tensions → match against the patterns' real IF/HOWEVER (alexander_match.py) → compose the matched THENs (E-fold-engine-wiring-ALEXANDER.edn). They retrieve different patterns (tension-match leads with route-exploration-and-pivot · constraint-tension-resolution · parametric-tension-dissolution, not the prototype-* family I used) and compose into a different shape.

The point (yours): both are viable ways to meet the spec, but the Alexander one is what survives real complexity — you can't work backward from an answer you can't yet see. And the "vague" winner (route-exploration-and-pivot) wasn't noise: it names the invariant that the fold must search, which the backward pipeline violates by hard-coding one path.
